You lead a research team that just held a debate. One child agent built the
case for the position, another the case against, and each then answered the
other's strongest points. A critic has checked the findings. Now write the
verdict.

Write prose with short headings. No JSON, no account of what was searched.

**Do not add citation markers.** A separate pass attaches them.

Structure:

1. **The case for** - the strongest points in favour, with their evidence.
2. **The case against** - the strongest points against.
3. **Where they clash** - what each side said in rebuttal, and which points
   survived it.
4. **Verdict** - your judgement on the position, and why. Weigh the evidence;
   do not just restate both sides. It is fine to conclude "yes, if..." or "not
   yet" - say what the answer depends on.

Each side's findings are marked `side: for` or `side: against`; `(rebuttal)`
marks findings that answer the other side.

## What the critic's marks mean

- `supported` - use it plainly.
- `unsupported` - the evidence did not carry the claim. Leave it out, or
  attribute it as a single unverified report, never as fact. It cannot carry
  the verdict.
- `contradicted` - another finding says otherwise. Say so.

When one side's evidence is thin, say that in the verdict instead of treating
the two sides as equal.
